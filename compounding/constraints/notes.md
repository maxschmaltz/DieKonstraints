# Notes

* Since we disregard weak M from research due to their absolute regularity, the corresponding evidence is excluded. Those are: [eisenberg_2013:228], [fuhrhop_kürschner_2015:573], [kürschner_2005:119], [kürschner_2010:9], [ortner_müller-bollhagen_1991:91], [schäfer_pankratz_2018:9].

* Since we consider only N+N compounds, we exclude constraints targeting complex first constituents. These are: 1) -s- occurs more frequently with complex first constituents compared to the corresponding simplex and sometimes replaces the default simplex linker ([koliopoulou_2014:61], [kürschner_2005:108,110,111,112], [ortner_müller-bollhagen_1991:85]), 2) the more complex the first part of a compound, the more probable the occurrence of a linking element ([nübling_szczepaniak_2008:9]); 3) -s- can signalize the head boundary ([kürschner_2005:112], [kürschner_2010:22], [nübling_szczepaniak_2008:8]); 4) the probability of -s- after a compound is comparable to that after derivatives with stressed prefixes and is about 27-37% ([nübling_szczepaniak_2008:21], [nübling_szczepaniak_2013:78]).

* The following fact is disregarded from the constraints despite its restraining nature: syllabic linkers attach only to final-stressed (including monosyllabic), and non-syllabic -- only to polysyllabic non-final-stressed nouns, which aids in forming/maintainign trochees: rule for -(e)n-, -es-, -e-, =e=, =er=, -(e)ns-, tendency for -s- (more on that in [kopf_2017:195], [kürschner_2010:17], [nübling_szczepaniak_2008:7,17], [nübling_szczepaniak_2013:79], [schlücker_2023:19], [wegener_2003:446]). The reason for disregarding this fact is its determinacy by other constraints: 1) none of these linkers can appear after a full vowel (see `p2l:phon_fin:vow$-0`), hence, they may appear either after a schwa, or after a consonant; 2) allomorphs of -(e)n-, -(e)ns- are distributed regularly depending on whether the noun ends with a schwa: thus, -n- and -ns- will appear only after schwa, otherwise -en-, -ens- (see constraints dealing with these linkers) (exception: F on -in (see `p2l:drv:sfx:F_#in$-en`), but there German avoids final -inn); 3) -e-, =e=, =er= are paradigmatic (see corresponding `l2p` constraints), and all e-plural, e=plural, er=plural nouns are consonant-final; 4) -es- appears only after a row of monosyllabic nouns (see `l2p:es`); 5) there are only a few monosyllabic nouns attaching an -s- (see `l2p:s|smpx_mn`). Thus, this fact rather summarizes the outcomes governed by other constraints but does not govern anything itself. Thus, it cannot bring any additional filter on any noun since it will always give the same output as the corresponding constraints provided above.

* Despite the findings from [krott_etal_2007] concerning analogical effects are promising, we disregard them as well from this iteration. These are: 1) there is a significant left bias for choosing a link (-en-, -s- and zero linker were investigated), that is, a noun tending to usually choose -x- will more probably also choose -x- in novel compounds [krott_etal_2007:7,9]; 2) apart from the left bias, gender was a significantly predictive feature for -en- and zero linker, and inflectional class -- on -s- and zero linker [krott_etal_2007:9]. The reason for excluding this findings is that we believe these analogical effects are an accumulative product of acquisition and (subconscious) application of all compounding constraints present in German. Such, it cannot be a constraint itself and is rather a combined account of the investigated constraints.

* We adhere to the framework that assigns to affixes a scheme defining the morphological and semantic properties of the input for derivation and its result. Thus, even if the morphological class of an input of a derivational constraint is restricted, we do not claim this constraint to also have a morphological character since we assume this morphological information is embedded in the affix. For example, we categorize `p2l:drv:sfx:F_#in$-en` or `p2l:drv:sfx:0_pl_sfx-0` as purely derivational constraint as this exact suffix -in takes certain Xs as the input and always produces an F with certain semantic properties, or suffixes -er, -el etc. always produce zero-plural nouns.

* When dealing with morphological constraints, simplices are meant by default. Otherwise, "derivational" type is specified in addition. This is, among other, the reason why `l2p:s|smpx_mn` is classified as purely morphological, and `l2p:s|f` -- as both morphological and derivational.
